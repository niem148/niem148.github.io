This project showcases the complete design and development process behind **The Wave Clock**, a layered wall clock designed and manufactured using a combination of Autodesk Fusion 360, 3ds Max, AI-assisted artwork generation and 3D printing.

The goal was to create a decorative clock featuring a layered mountain and wave landscape while ensuring the design remained practical to manufacture on a standard-sized print bed. From the initial concept through to final print profiles, every component was designed, tested and refined to create a product that is both visually appealing and easy for others to print and assemble.

## Key Features

- **Interchangeable Inner Scene**: The internal layered landscape can be swapped for alternative designs.
- **Multi-Layer Depth Effect**: Four separate scene layers create depth and shadow within the clock face.
- **Print-Bed Optimised Design**: Every layer was divided into manageable sections to fit standard 3D printer build volumes.
- **Hidden Dowel Assembly System**: Dowel joints allow the printed sections to be accurately aligned and securely joined.
- **Optimised Bambu Studio Profiles**: Includes prepared print settings balancing quality and print speed.
- **Integrated Wall Hanging Mount**: Built directly into the rear shell for simple installation.

<br><br>

## Materials & Components

- **Sunlu HS Marble PLA** – Used for the outer shell and decorative clock components
- **Clock Mechanism Kit** – Silent quartz movement and clock shaft
- **Wooden Clock Hands** – Lightweight hands to suit the design aesthetic
- **Copper Spray Paint** – Applied to the clock markers and hands
- **Alignment Dowels** – Used to reconnect large model sections after printing
- **Superglue** – For final assembly and securing joined components

## Design Process

### Initial Concept

The mountain and wave artwork was initially generated using **Gemini AI** to explore different landscape compositions. Once I settled on a design I liked, the artwork was imported into **3ds Max** where the real development work began.

The original artwork was converted into a manufacturable model and separated into four independent depth layers, allowing the final clock face to create a realistic layered landscape effect.

### Model Preparation in 3ds Max

Each of the four design layers exceeded the printable area of the target print bed.

To overcome this:

1. The artwork was separated into four individual depth layers.
2. Each layer was split into two printable sections.
3. Alignment features were added to ensure accurate positioning.
4. Dowel joints were designed to reconnect the sections after printing.
5. The completed assemblies were prepared for import into Fusion 360.

This approach allowed a large decorative scene to be produced on a standard consumer 3D printer without compromising the overall design.

<br><br>

## Fusion 360 Development

After the layers were prepared in 3ds Max, they were imported into **Autodesk Fusion 360**.

Fusion was used to:

- Design all four internal support rings
- Create locating features for each landscape layer
- Develop the outer clock shell
- Integrate the clock mechanism cavity
- Create the hanging hole for wall mounting
- Verify clearances and assembly tolerances
- Validate overall dimensions before production

Several prototype iterations were printed during development to ensure all parts fitted together reliably.

## 3D Printing & Optimisation

The clock was prepared using **Bambu Studio**, where extensive testing and optimisation was carried out.

Key development work included:

- Adjusting assembly tolerances between rings and inserts
- Testing support placement for easier post-processing
- Optimising layer height settings
- Evaluating print quality versus print time
- Validating dowel fitment accuracy
- Organising print plates for efficient production

A complete project file was created containing:

- Organised build plates
- Printer configurations
- Material settings
- Three preset slicing profiles for different quality and speed requirements

The final arrangement allows users to quickly print the complete clock without needing to organise parts manually.

<br><br>

## Troubleshooting & Iterations

Like most product development projects, the design went through several iterations before reaching the final version.

Some of the challenges included:

- **Tolerance Adjustments**: Ring clearances and insert fitment required multiple test prints.
- **Dowel Accuracy**: Several dowel sizes were tested to achieve reliable alignment without excessive force.
- **Large Part Splitting**: Parts had to be carefully divided to minimise visible seams after assembly.
- **Support Optimisation**: Support structures were redesigned to improve print quality and reduce clean-up time.
- **Assembly Strength**: Joint locations were refined to maximise strength while remaining easy to assemble.

Each iteration improved both manufacturing reliability and overall product quality.

## AI Assistance

AI tools played an important role during the concept phase of the project.

**Gemini** was used to generate and refine landscape concepts that inspired the final wave and mountain scene. The generated artwork served as a starting point before being manually reconstructed, split and engineered for manufacturing in 3ds Max and Fusion 360.

AI accelerated the creative process, while all engineering, modelling, assembly design, tolerance testing and print optimisation were completed manually.

<br><br>

## Conclusion

The Wave Clock demonstrates a complete product development workflow that combines AI-assisted concept generation, CAD modelling, mesh editing, additive manufacturing and iterative engineering.

From converting AI artwork into a manufacturable design, through multi-part assembly development, tolerance testing and print optimisation, the project showcases both creative and technical problem-solving skills.

The result is a decorative wall clock that is visually striking, practical to manufacture and easy to reproduce using the supplied print profiles and organised production workflow.
